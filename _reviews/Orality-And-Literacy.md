## Notes
- the primary form of language is verbal, not written
- the idea of'study' being a byproduct of writing, further to writing is thinking
- Homeric question and the idea that oral "texts" are made up of formulaic or formulary parts
- almost exactly repeated or set ideas are back in electronic culture but were explicitly taught against in print 
- sound is dynamic, never static, in contrast with basically all other senses (except touch?)
- oral though is formulaic and pattern based to compress more ideas into memorable chunks for recall - more for the speaker (i.e. thinker) than the listener
- words used in oral cultures are only those relevant to present life, archaic or irrelevant words drop out of usage without preservation in eg a dictionary - history will change if no longer relevant to the present audience (ref Ted Chiang story about missionary / writing in south American culture -"The Truth of Fact, the Truth of Feeling")
- characters are by necessity more memorable
- writing as a technology that extends thought 
- AI is not necessary to critique itself
- argues that spoken words exist in the context they're uttered in (true) and that written words are isolated - but they exist in the context of the corpus and any other previously written words
- scholarly, not mother-tongue and primarily written languages for education, e.g. scholarly Latin, and the effect of removing the oral and emotional context 
- from printing press, change in books from recordings to containers - books now contained information rather than just capturing an utterance 
- dictionaries (and therefore the languages they aim to define) are based on the written, rather than the oral - oral or vernacular usage of e.g. English is now seen as "wrong" if it deviates from the written usage
- rediscovery of intertextuality after romantic ideas of creativity and originality, may be why it is such a powerful tool today
- linear plots (and then later intentional non-linearity) are a byproduct of literacy and weren't considered in oral texts
- similar to rounded characters as opposed to larger than life characters needed to be memorable as part of oral texts 
- narrative as a sense making tool stems from oral cultures because they don't have a way of storing abstract categories or recording things for later outside of in a memorable narrative
