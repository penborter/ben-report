I realised recently that very few (if any) of the photos on this website have alt text, i.e. the text that displays when the image won't or that's used by blind readers to understand the image textually.

So, I'm going through the process of adding alt text to images in posts, which is mostly straightforward, except when we get to the photos on the [Photos](/photos) pages of the site.
These are "artistic" images; they don't serve any purpose beyond the content of the image itself. The point is the image itself, rather than some specific meaning conveyed by the image in the context of a broader post or surrounding text.

But what does an image mean in the context of the overall page if the page is essentially the image itself[^1]? And how can you represent the function of an image that serves only an artistic function?

{% include quote.html 
content='What if our goal is to communicate, in alt text, the visual experience of an image, and help blind users know what it is like to see an image? Conceived this way, the point of alt text is not to allow blind readers to access information communicated visually, but to allow them to access visual imagery, even if not visually.'
author='Aaron Richardson | Aesthetics for Birds'
title='Alt Text for Artwork, Alt Text as Artwork'
url='https://aestheticsforbirds.com/2024/05/02/how-to-write-alt-text/'
%}

As you can imagine, this is a (somewhat) solved problem already; a lot of work has been put [a lot of work](https://aestheticsforbirds.com/2024/05/02/how-to-write-alt-text/) into [thinking about](https://aestheticsforbirds.com/2022/09/15/accessibility-and-the-problem-of-alt-text-who-is-it-for-and-how-could-it-be-better/) [what it means](https://www.cooperhewitt.org/cooper-hewitt-guidelines-for-image-description/) to [write alt text for art](https://www.nga.gov/visit/accessibility/collection-image-descriptions). 

[^1]: Obviously most of the images in the photos pages are in galleries, but you know what I mean.
